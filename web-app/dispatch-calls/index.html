Dispatch & Calls

Calls are the incidents, jobs, events and tasks your people respond to. Dispatching a call tells the right people and units about it — by push notification, text, email and voice — tracks who is responding and who is on scene, collects notes, photos and files, and closes with a disposition that feeds your records and reports.

Where to find it​

Left menu → Calls. The Calls page shows:

  • Unit statuses — every unit with its current state and when it changed.
  • Active calls — open calls with number, name, priority and time, plus View, Update and Close buttons.
  • A map of active calls and units.
  • Pending calls — a panel above Active calls, shown while any call is waiting for a dispatcher (see Pending and scheduled calls).
  • Buttons for Routes, Archived calls, Pending calls and Scheduled calls (each with a count badge while calls are waiting) and New Call.

Creating a call​

New Call (the same form is used by the Dispatch app and by email/SMS import).

PriorityLow, Medium, High, Emergency or your custom priorities. Priority decides the notification sound, colour and whether the call overrides quiet hours in the apps.
TypeFire, Medical, Rescue … from your call types. Type drives protocols, run cards, NERIS crosswalk and reporting.
Reporter name / contact infoWho called it in.
Primary / additional contactsLink Contacts (a premises, a client, a company). Contacts with alert notes pop up a warning.
Call identifier / Incident Id / Reference IdExternal CAD, parent incident and partner numbers for cross-referencing.
Nature of the callThe dispatch text; rich text supported.
NotesAdditional notes.
Map pageMap-book reference.
LocationType an address and Find Address, enter a what3words address, enter latitude/longitude, or Set pin on map. Reverse geocoding fills the address from a pin. A call saved with an address but no pin is geocoded when it is created, so run cards and maps still get a location.
DestinationA point of interest such as a hospital, shelter, transfer centre or staging area (from Mapping → POIs).
Indoor locationA zone from an indoor map.

Who gets dispatched​

Tick any combination of:

TargetEffect
PersonnelNamed members. The grid shows ETA to the call, current status, staffing and roles.
GroupsEvery member of a station or group. With Dispatch shift instead of group on (Department Settings), only members signed up for today's shift are dispatched.
RolesEvery member holding a role (e.g. Driver, Paramedic).
UnitsApparatus and their crews.

If Run Cards are enabled, the recommendation for the call's priority/type is pre-filled here (or dispatched automatically). The recommendation panel refreshes when you change the priority or type and again as soon as the call is placed (Find Address, what3words, a map click or dragging the pin), so you can pick the type first and the location second. Units it recommends show their distance and, in closest-unit mode, their response time in minutes.

Nearest units​

Once the call has a location, the Nearest Units table lists every unit you may see (a unit can be a team, an apparatus or an individual):

ColumnShows
UnitName, unit type, station and service area, and a badge when the call is inside the unit's own (or parent) boundary.
StatusCurrent status, green when available.
ETATime to the call. When the department sets turnout times it is the full response time, with incl. N s turnout underneath. est. marks a straight-line estimate; without the mark it is a routed drive time (Dispatch Settings → Order by driving ETA). Distance in km below.
PositionLive GPS or Station location. A unit in an In Quarters status, or with no GPS position, is placed at its station. A GPS position older than the maximum location age is marked stale.
Shift / Role mixCrew on shift and the roles they hold.

Available units come first, ordered by response time — the fastest unit is at the top. A stale position is flagged on the row but does not push the unit down the list. Units with no position at all, and unavailable units, come last.

The call map draws only the available units and personnel, colored by their status, so it shows who can be sent. Busy and unavailable resources are left off the map but stay in the dispatch lists with their status, so a dispatcher can still pick one to reassign. Update Call and the Dispatch app's New Call and Edit Call maps work the same way.

Press Create Call. Notifications go out immediately through the queue: push to the apps, SMS, email and voice according to each member's notification preferences and contact verification status. Notification text is generic when the department is enrolled in Advanced Data Protection. A call saved as pending or scheduled for later sends nothing until it is dispatched, and opens on the Pending calls page instead of the Calls dashboard.

Working an active call​

View opens the call page.

PanelWhat you can do
Call detailsEverything entered above, with the dispatch audio if the call came from a radio/TTS source.
MapCall location, responding personnel and units live, route lines.
Dispatched personnel / unitsWho was dispatched, their current status and timestamps (responding, on scene …).
NotesAdd notes (with your location if allowed); flag a note for follow-up with a reason.
Images and filesAttach photos, documents, audio and video up to 10 MB; flag inappropriate items for moderation.
Check-in timersPAR / rehab / IC / hazmat countdowns with Check In buttons and history.
Incident reportStart the NERIS incident report or a Run record.
ExportPrintable call export, PDF, and a shareable anonymous export link.

Update changes any field, adds or removes dispatch targets (only the differences are applied) and can rebroadcast the notification. When the department uses Run Cards, a Run card panel above the dispatch lists shows what the call's run card still needs at its current alarm level, after the units and people already on the call; it follows the priority, type and location on the form, so changing them before saving updates it. Select recommended ticks those units and people in the lists; nothing is ticked on its own, so saving another change never adds resources, and nothing is sent until you save. Close asks for a close state (Completed, Cancelled, Unfounded, False alarm …) and closing notes. Tick Send notification to tell everyone on the call that it is closed: the personnel, groups and roles it was dispatched to (Responder app, text and email per their notification settings), its units (Unit app) and the incident command team — commander, people holding incident roles, and the people and units on the command board (Incident Command app). The notice names the call and how it was closed; the closing notes are not sent. Closed calls move to Archived calls and can be re-opened.

A call that is being run under an active incident command cannot be closed from here, the API, the chatbot or the auto-close job: close the command first. In the Incident Command app, End Command has an Also close the call option (with its own Notify everyone on the call switch) that closes the call right after the command; offline, both are queued and the call closes only once the command has.

Closing a call automatically​

With Close call when last unit clears on (Dispatch Settings, off by default), an active call closes on its own when the last unit dispatched to it goes from working the call to back in service or out of service: a status whose base type is Available, In Quarters, Cleared, Completed or On Patrol, or Unavailable, Not Responding or Maintenance. An ambulance going On Scene, Departing to Hospital, Arrived Hospital, Radio Available closes the call when it reports Radio Available, as long as every other unit on the call has also cleared.

The call stays open for the dispatcher to close when:

  • another unit on the call is still working it (returning, transporting, at the hospital), or has not reported a status since it was dispatched;
  • the call has an active incident command, or no unit was dispatched to it (only units count, not personnel, groups or roles);
  • the status has no base type, or the unit was not working the call (an available unit going out of service closes nothing);
  • the status was set by Resgrid itself (dispatch and release statuses), or is an offline status replayed later.

The call is closed as Closed with a note naming the last unit, by the member who set that status. Release statuses are applied as on any close (units that cleared keep their statuses), the Call Closed workflow trigger fires, and nobody is notified. Calls where units skip statuses (no patient found, false alarm, patient deceased on scene) are still closed by the dispatcher.

Closing a call, by hand or automatically, never overrides a unit someone took out of service: the release status only moves units still working the call.

Delete soft-deletes a call with a reason (it can be restored by support).

Which call a status belongs to​

Crews often set a status without choosing a call: they tap On Scene and carry on. Resgrid then puts the status on the call the unit or person is working, so it shows on that call's record and counts toward its times. In order:

  1. A call they chose is kept.

  2. The call of their previous status, while that call is open and the previous status did not clear it. On Scene after Responding to call 26-153 goes on 26-153, and so does the Available that ends it (that records when the unit cleared).

  3. The one open call they are dispatched to, for a status that is not itself a clear. Calls their last clear already ended are left out:

    • a clear that named a call ended that call;
    • a clear that named no call (back in quarters, available at a station, or no destination at all) ended every call they had been sent to before it. Only a dispatch, or a re-dispatch, after that clear is new work.

    With more than one call left, the status is not linked to any.

A clear is a status whose base type is Available, In Quarters, Completed, On Patrol, Cleared, Returning, Unavailable, Not Responding or Maintenance, plus the built-in unit statuses Available, Unavailable, Out of Service, Returning, Cancelled and Released and the built-in personnel statuses Available, Not Responding and Available Station.

For example, Medic 12 is dispatched to 26-153, clears In Quarters at its station while 26-153 stays open for the engine, then reports Responding without picking a call. That status is not put on 26-153. If Medic 12 has been dispatched to 26-160 since it cleared, the status goes on 26-160.

Some statuses are placed differently:

  • A person put on a unit by a unit status goes on that unit status's call.
  • A scheduled call counts only once it has gone out or its dispatch time has passed, and a pending call only once it is dispatched. Statuses set before then are not linked to it.
  • A status replayed from an app's offline queue skips steps 2 and 3 when it arrives. The call record still shows statuses without a call that a dispatched unit or person set while working it, placed by their own time.

The same rules pick the call a unit's next status starts on in the Dispatch app.

Pending and scheduled calls​

Two kinds of call can wait before anyone is notified:

  • Pending calls have no dispatch time. They wait until a dispatcher picks one up, chooses who to send it to and dispatches it — the typical use is follow-up work fed in from another system (a case-management or scheduling system, a referral line) or calls taken now for a team to pick up later.
  • Scheduled calls have a dispatch time and are sent automatically when it arrives.

Both show as count badges on the Pending calls and Scheduled calls buttons of the Calls dashboard, and pending calls also get a panel above Active calls (see the screenshot at the top of this page).

How a call becomes pending: tick Save as pending on New Call (web or the Dispatch app), or create it through the API with "IsPending": true (see Technical reference). A pending call is numbered and saved like any other call, but:

  • nobody is notified, and dispatch statuses are not applied;
  • it does not appear in the active calls lists, on maps, the Big Board or the Responder and Unit apps;
  • it does not count as active or archived; it is listed only under Pending calls;
  • the New Call form's dispatch-list and dispatch-time requirements (Department Settings → New call form fields) do not apply to it — the dispatcher chooses recipients when sending it; every other required field still does.

Pending calls page (the Pending Calls button on the Calls page, or View all on the dashboard panel) lists waiting calls oldest first with View, Update, Dispatch Now, Close and Delete.

Working a pending call

ActionWhat happens
ViewThe call page shows a Pending banner and a Dispatch Now button; Re-Open and Strike Alarm are not offered.
UpdateEdit any field and pick who to send it to. Saving keeps it pending and notifies nobody; Save and Dispatch Now saves and sends it in one step.
Dispatch NowSends the call to everyone on its dispatch list. If the list is empty you are taken to Update to choose recipients first.
ScheduleGive it a Schedule dispatch time on Update: it becomes a scheduled call.
CloseCancel it (or close it with any close state) without notifying anyone.

When a waiting call is dispatched it becomes an active call, its dispatch time is recorded separately from the time it was received, run card auto-dispatch is applied, notifications go out, and dispatch statuses are set. For a pending call this is also when the Call Added workflow trigger fires and the call's incident chat channel is created (when chat is on), not when it was saved.

Scheduled calls page (the Scheduled Calls button on the Calls page) lists calls waiting for their time, with View, Update, Dispatch Now (send early), Close and Delete. A scheduled call is sent within five minutes of its time by the scheduled-calls job. Adding or removing recipients before then notifies nobody — the updated list is used when it goes out. Until then the units and people on its list are not on the call: a status they set is not linked to it, and it is not the call a unit's next status starts on in the Dispatch app. A scheduled call closed before its time is never sent.

The Dispatch app has the same two lists, with tiles on its console.

Archived calls​

Archived calls — closed calls by year, with view and export. Add archived call creates a historical call with a custom date and pre-set closed state (optionally recalculating the year's call numbers with your department's call number pattern; not available for a pattern with no year) — useful when importing history or logging a call that happened outside Resgrid. New calls are numbered with that pattern too. Pending calls are never listed here.
