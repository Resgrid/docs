Dispatch & Calls

Calls are the incidents, jobs, events and tasks your people respond to. Dispatching a call tells the right people and units about it — by push notification, text, email and voice — tracks who is responding and who is on scene, collects notes, photos and files, and closes with a disposition that feeds your records and reports.

Where to find it​

Left menu → Calls. The Calls page shows:

  • Unit statuses — every unit with its current state and when it changed.
  • Active calls — open calls with number, name, priority and time, plus View, Update and Close buttons.
  • A map of active calls and units.
  • Pending calls — a panel above Active calls, shown while any call is waiting for a dispatcher (see Pending and scheduled calls).
  • Buttons for Routes, Archived calls, Pending calls and Scheduled calls (each with a count badge while calls are waiting) and New Call.

Creating a call​

New Call (the same form is used by the Dispatch app and by email/SMS import).

Your department decides which of these fields and sections the form shows and which are required (New call form fields); a hidden one is simply not on the form. The Template button, the contact pickers and Type only appear when the department has templates, contacts or call types.

Who gets dispatched​

Tick any combination of the lists your department shows:

TargetEffect
PersonnelNamed members. The grid shows ETA to the call, current status, staffing and roles.
GroupsEvery member of a station or group. With Dispatch shift instead of group on (Department Settings), only members signed up for today's shift are dispatched.
RolesEvery member holding a role (e.g. Driver, Paramedic).
UnitsApparatus and their crews.

If Run Cards are enabled, the recommendation for the call's priority/type is pre-filled here (or dispatched automatically). The recommendation panel refreshes when you change the priority or type and again as soon as the call is placed (Find Address, what3words, a map click or dragging the pin), so you can pick the type first and the location second. Units it recommends show their distance and, in closest-unit mode, their response time in minutes.

When the department hides every list. The form shows All Call in place of the lists. Leave it off to save an informational call: it is created and listed like any other call, but pages nobody (run cards that dispatch automatically still send their units). Switch it on to send the call to everyone in the department. On Update Call, All Call adds everyone to the call's recipients; left off, the call keeps the recipients it has.

A call only goes to everyone when someone chooses that: All Call here, Everyone in the apps, or "0" through the API. A call created with nobody picked (for example from an older app whose picker the department hid) pages nobody.

Nearest units​

Once the call has a location, the Nearest Units table lists every unit you may see (a unit can be a team, an apparatus or an individual):

ColumnShows
UnitName, unit type, station and service area, and a badge when the call is inside the unit's own (or parent) boundary.
StatusCurrent status, green when available.
ETATime to the call. When the department sets turnout times it is the full response time, with incl. N s turnout underneath. est. marks a straight-line estimate; without the mark it is a routed drive time (Dispatch Settings → Order by driving ETA, see below for which units get one). Distance in km below.
PositionLive GPS or Station location. A unit in an In Quarters status, or with no GPS position, is placed at its station. A GPS position older than the maximum location age is marked stale.
Shift / Role mixCrew on shift and the roles they hold.

Available units come first, in the order the department ranks closest units (Dispatch Settings → Rank closest units by): by response time by default, so the fastest unit is at the top, or by straight-line distance. A stale position is flagged on the row but does not push the unit down the list. Units with no position at all, and unavailable units, come last.

Drive times. With Order by driving ETA on, Resgrid looks up road drive times the same way a run card does:

  • Who gets one: the ETA shortlist size best available units of each unit type (so ambulances and MUGs are each looked up, not just the nearest units overall), plus the closest responders.
  • Shared positions: units at the same position, such as two ambulances in quarters at one station, share one lookup. A call makes at most 25 lookups.
  • Order within a type: units with a looked-up drive time come before units with only an estimate, as in a run card. The first unit of each type is the one a closest-unit run card would pick for it, unless the run card skips it for staffing or a rest period.
  • Order across types: the types are merged by response time (or distance).
  • The note under the table says how many positions were looked up.

In Shortest distance mode the drive times are still shown, but they don't change the order.

Travel modes and road closures. Each unit is measured the way it travels (travel mode), and routed times go around or through the department's active road closures:

  • A unit that cycles, walks or flies shows a bicycle, walker or plane icon and the mode's name under its time. Driving units show nothing extra and rank exactly as before.
  • A cycling or walking unit's drive time is routed on cycle routes or footpaths. Units at the same position share a lookup only when they travel the same way, so a foot team and an ambulance at one station are each looked up.
  • An air unit's time is the straight line at its air speed. It is never routed, so it carries no est. mark and uses none of the lookups.
  • When road closures added time to a unit's route (a slowdown, or a detour the routing provider could not take), the row shows +N min for road closures; hover it for the closure names. A route that went around closures without added time shows N road closure(s) avoided.
  • The note under the table names the closures the drive times account for.

Responders are always measured as driving. The Dispatch app's unit picker ranks units the same way.

The call map draws only the available units and personnel, colored by their status, so it shows who can be sent. Busy and unavailable resources are left off the map but stay in the dispatch lists with their status, so a dispatcher can still pick one to reassign. Update Call and the Dispatch app's New Call and Edit Call maps work the same way.

Where the installation has live traffic, the New Call, Update Call and Add Archived Call maps show it from the start; the car button on the map turns it off, and your choice is remembered on this device (Live traffic).

Press Create Call. Notifications go out immediately through the queue: push to the apps, SMS, email and voice according to each member's notification preferences and contact verification status. Notification text is generic when the department is enrolled in Advanced Data Protection. A call saved as pending or scheduled for later sends nothing until it is dispatched, and opens on the Pending calls page instead of the Calls dashboard.

Working an active call​

View opens the call page.

Update changes any field, adds or removes dispatch targets (only the differences are applied) and can rebroadcast the notification. A unit or person released from the call stays on its record and is not ticked on the form; ticking them again sends them back. When the department uses Run Cards, a Run card panel above the dispatch lists shows what the call's run card still needs at its current alarm level, after the units and people already on the call; it follows the priority, type and location on the form, so changing them before saving updates it. Select recommended ticks those units and people in the lists; nothing is ticked on its own, so saving another change never adds resources, and nothing is sent until you save. Close asks for a close state (Completed, Cancelled, Unfounded, False alarm …) and closing notes. Tick Send notification to tell everyone on the call that it is closed: the personnel, groups and roles it was dispatched to (Responder app, text and email per their notification settings), its units (Unit app) and the incident command team — commander, people holding incident roles, and the people and units on the command board (Incident Command app). The notice names the call and how it was closed; the closing notes are not sent. Closed calls move to Archived calls and can be re-opened.

A call that is being run under an active incident command cannot be closed from here, the API, the chatbot or the auto-close job: close the command first. In the Incident Command app, End Command has an Also close the call option (with its own Notify everyone on the call switch) that closes the call right after the command; offline, both are queued and the call closes only once the command has.

Closing a call automatically​

With Close call when last unit clears on (Dispatch Settings, off by default), an active call closes on its own when the last unit dispatched to it goes from working the call to back in service or out of service: a status whose base type is Available, In Quarters, Cleared, Completed or On Patrol, or Unavailable, Not Responding or Maintenance. An ambulance going On Scene, Departing to Hospital, Arrived Hospital, Radio Available closes the call when it reports Radio Available, as long as every other unit on the call has also cleared.

The call stays open for the dispatcher to close when:

  • another unit on the call is still working it (returning, transporting, at the hospital), or has not reported a status since it was dispatched. A unit released from the call doesn't count, whatever it is doing now;
  • the call has an active incident command, or no unit was dispatched to it (only units count, not personnel, groups or roles);
  • the status has no base type, or the unit was not working the call (an available unit going out of service closes nothing);
  • the status was set by Resgrid itself (dispatch and release statuses), or is an offline status replayed later.

The call is closed as Closed with a note naming the last unit, by the member who set that status. Release statuses are applied as on any close (units that cleared keep their statuses), the Call Closed workflow trigger fires, and nobody is notified. Calls where units skip statuses (no patient found, false alarm, patient deceased on scene) are still closed by the dispatcher.

Closing a call, by hand or automatically, never overrides a unit someone took out of service: the release status only moves units still working the call.

Delete soft-deletes a call with a reason (it can be restored by support).

Releasing a unit or person from a call​

Sometimes a resource is no longer needed but the call goes on. The ambulance arrives first and stands the MUG (the emergency physician car) down before it gets there, or a second engine is turned back because the first has it in hand. Release takes that one unit or person off the call without closing it, so they are free for the next call.

Where to find it:

  • View Call (web): a Release button beside each unit and person on the call.
  • Dispatch app (Omni): the − button on a unit or person row while the console is filtered to a call, and on the call's Dispatched tab. The Incident Command app's Dispatched tab has it too.
  • Unit app (Omni): Release from call on the active call card releases the crew's own unit.

Confirm the release. With Notify on (the default), the unit or person gets a Dispatch Cancelled notice for the call: the unit's devices, or the person by their notification settings.

What a release does:

  • Status. The unit gets the department's Unit Call Release Status. A person gets the Person Call Release Status (Dispatch Settings) when one is set, otherwise the department's Available status. As when a call closes, a unit or person keeps their status if they are already back in service, out of service or unavailable, or are working another open call. A MUG crew that already reported Radio Available stays Radio Available.
  • Off the call. The unit or person leaves the call's lists: the dispatched resources on View Call, and the Units on call and Personnel on call lists in the apps. A person reached through a dispatched group or role is left out too, even though the group or role stays on the call.
  • Free for other calls. From then on Resgrid treats them as off the call:
    • a rebroadcast or dispatch re-notification doesn't page them again;
    • they don't get the call-closed notice, and the call's check-in timers no longer wait for them;
    • they no longer count toward the call's run card requirements, so they can be recommended again;
    • they don't hold the call open for Closing a call automatically;
    • they drop out of the calls I'm on badge and the active calls counts;
    • their next statuses are not put back on the call (see Which call a status belongs to).
  • Kept on the record.
    • The call's history shows that the unit or person was released, and when.
    • The unit's dispatch time stays on the call.
    • The Call Unit Times report has a Released column.
    • An incident report started from the call fills canceled en route for a unit that was released before it arrived.

Who can release:

  • A unit: anyone who may edit the call, or the unit's own crew (anyone allowed to set that unit's status).
  • A person: anyone who may edit the call, a department admin, or the person themselves.

Only an open call that has gone out has resources to release. A pending or scheduled call is changed by editing its dispatch list.

Sending them back. Tick the unit or person again on Update (or the Dispatch app's call edit, or add them as a resource) and save. That is a new dispatch: they are paged and get the dispatch status as usual. A person released from a call comes back only when they are picked by name. Paging their group or role again doesn't bring them back.

Transport destinations​

A call's Destination (a hospital or other destination POI, chosen when the call is created or edited) is where its patient or load is taken. When a unit's status has the base type Transporting or At Hospital and the status offers points of interest as destinations, the Omni, Unit and Dispatch apps pre-select the call's destination instead of the call, marked Assigned to call in the list. The crew or dispatcher only confirms it. The call's destination must be a destination POI the status offers; otherwise the status starts on the call as before. A unit already transporting to a hospital (its current status is Transporting or At Hospital with that hospital) starts on that hospital instead, so a crew that changed hospital on Departing to Hospital keeps it for Arrived Hospital.

Choosing another point of interest shows Differs from the call's assigned destination with the call's destination, so the dispatcher can confirm the change by radio. In the Omni dispatch console, a unit whose transport status points somewhere other than its call's destination shows the call's destination in red under its row.

With Transport destination checks on (Dispatch Settings, off by default):

  • choosing another destination needs a reason, entered as the status note (STEMI for a cath lab, stroke centre, trauma centre, emergency department closed). Arriving at the hospital the unit already reported does not ask again;
  • Resgrid adds a note to the call naming the unit, the status, both destinations and the reason, for example ZW Herzele set Vertrek ZH to UZ Gent, not the call's assigned destination AZ Sint-Elisabeth Zottegem. Reason: STEMI, cath lab. A unit that then reports At Hospital at the same place is not noted again. A status from an app version that predates this setting is saved without asking, and its note says no reason was given.

When the crew asks for a different hospital ("the nearest cath lab"), the destination step's points of interest can be narrowed to one capability. With the unit's position known they are ranked nearest first: the closest by drive time (the unit's own travel mode and vehicle limits apply), destinations on diversion or closed after those that can take the patient. The call form's destination picker ranks the same way from the call's location.

The call's destination itself does not change: it is the one dispatch assigned, and each unit's status shows where that unit went. A status pointing at a hospital is no longer on the call's record; the Call Unit Times report still includes it.

Which call a status belongs to​

Crews often set a status without choosing a call: they tap On Scene and carry on. Resgrid then puts the status on the call the unit or person is working, so it shows on that call's record and counts toward its times. In order:

  1. A call they chose is kept.

  2. The call of their previous status, while that call is open and the previous status did not clear it. On Scene after Responding to call 26-153 goes on 26-153, and so does the Available that ends it (that records when the unit cleared).

  3. The one open call they are dispatched to, for a status that is not itself a clear. Calls their last clear already ended are left out:

    • a clear that named a call ended that call;
    • a clear that named no call (back in quarters, available at a station, or no destination at all) ended every call they had been sent to before it. Only a dispatch, or a re-dispatch, after that clear is new work.

    With more than one call left, the status is not linked to any.

A clear is a status whose base type is Available, In Quarters, Completed, On Patrol, Cleared, Returning, Unavailable, Not Responding or Maintenance, plus the built-in unit statuses Available, Unavailable, Out of Service, Returning, Cancelled and Released and the built-in personnel statuses Available, Not Responding and Available Station.

For example, Medic 12 is dispatched to 26-153, clears In Quarters at its station while 26-153 stays open for the engine, then reports Responding without picking a call. That status is not put on 26-153. If Medic 12 has been dispatched to 26-160 since it cleared, the status goes on 26-160.

Some statuses are placed differently:

  • A person put on a unit by a unit status goes on that unit status's call.
  • A unit or person released from a call is off it: steps 2 and 3 never put a later status back on that call, and the call's record of them ends at the release.
  • A scheduled call counts only once it has gone out or its dispatch time has passed, and a pending call only once it is dispatched. Statuses set before then are not linked to it.
  • A status replayed from an app's offline queue skips steps 2 and 3 when it arrives. The call record still shows statuses without a call that a dispatched unit or person set while working it, placed by their own time.

The same rules pick the call a unit's next status starts on in the Dispatch app.

Pending and scheduled calls​

Two kinds of call can wait before anyone is notified:

  • Pending calls have no dispatch time. They wait until a dispatcher picks one up, chooses who to send it to and dispatches it — the typical use is follow-up work fed in from another system (a case-management or scheduling system, a referral line) or calls taken now for a team to pick up later.
  • Scheduled calls have a dispatch time and are sent automatically when it arrives.

Both show as count badges on the Pending calls and Scheduled calls buttons of the Calls dashboard, and pending calls also get a panel above Active calls (see the screenshot at the top of this page).

How a call becomes pending: tick Save as pending on New Call (web or the Dispatch app), or create it through the API with "IsPending": true (see Technical reference). A pending call is numbered and saved like any other call, but:

  • nobody is notified, and dispatch statuses are not applied;
  • it does not appear in the active calls lists, on maps, the Big Board or the Responder and Unit apps;
  • it does not count as active or archived; it is listed only under Pending calls;
  • the New Call form's dispatch-list and dispatch-time requirements (Department Settings → New call form fields) do not apply to it — the dispatcher chooses recipients when sending it; every other required field still does.

Pending calls page (the Pending Calls button on the Calls page, or View all on the dashboard panel) lists waiting calls oldest first with View, Update, Dispatch Now, Close and Delete.

Working a pending call

ActionWhat happens
ViewThe call page shows a Pending banner and a Dispatch Now button; Re-Open and Strike Alarm are not offered.
UpdateEdit any field and pick who to send it to. Saving keeps it pending and notifies nobody; Save and Dispatch Now saves and sends it in one step.
Dispatch NowSends the call to everyone on its dispatch list. If the list is empty you are taken to Update to choose recipients first.
ScheduleGive it a Schedule dispatch time on Update: it becomes a scheduled call.
CloseCancel it (or close it with any close state) without notifying anyone.

When a waiting call is dispatched it becomes an active call, its dispatch time is recorded separately from the time it was received, run card auto-dispatch is applied, notifications go out, and dispatch statuses are set. For a pending call this is also when the Call Added workflow trigger fires and the call's incident chat channel is created (when chat is on), not when it was saved.

Scheduled calls page (the Scheduled Calls button on the Calls page) lists calls waiting for their time, with View, Update, Dispatch Now (send early), Close and Delete. A scheduled call is sent within five minutes of its time by the scheduled-calls job. Adding or removing recipients before then notifies nobody — the updated list is used when it goes out. Until then the units and people on its list are not on the call: a status they set is not linked to it, and it is not the call a unit's next status starts on in the Dispatch app. A scheduled call closed before its time is never sent.

The Dispatch app has the same two lists, with tiles on its console.

Calendar of scheduled calls​

The Calendar tab on the Scheduled calls page shows every scheduled call waiting to go out, the calls recurring calls have created and their runs still to come, waiting for a dispatcher, held or missed, by month, week, day or as a list. See Scheduled & Recurring Calls.

The department calendar has a Show scheduled calls box that adds the same entries to it, for members who can see calls.

Recurring calls​

A recurring call is a call dispatched on a schedule — for example interfacility transfers every Tuesday and Thursday at 08:00, 13:00 and 19:00. Resgrid creates each call ahead of its time (or alerts a dispatcher to send it), sends it to the units and people you chose when it is due, leaves off those who are not available, holds the call for a dispatcher when nobody is left, and alerts the dispatchers when something needs fixing. Open Recurring Calls from the Scheduled calls page, or press Repeat on any call to start one filled in from that call. Everything about them — the form, pausing, alerts, the Dispatch app, workflows and the API — is on Scheduled & Recurring Calls.

Archived calls​

Archived calls — closed calls by year, with view and export. Add archived call creates a historical call with a custom date and pre-set closed state (optionally recalculating the year's call numbers with your department's call number pattern; not available for a pattern with no year) — useful when importing history or logging a call that happened outside Resgrid. New calls are numbered with that pattern too. Pending calls are never listed here.

APIapi/v4/Calls/*. DispatchList: "0" is everyone and the only way to ask for it; on SaveCall a blank list or "\|" dispatches nobody, on EditCall a blank list keeps the recipients and "0" adds everyone. MapPage and ContactId left out (null) are not held to the field rules. Pending and scheduled: POST SaveCall with IsPending (empty DispatchList = no proposed recipients; "0" = everyone; DispatchOn ignored), GET GetPendingCalls, PUT DispatchCallNow { "CallId", "DispatchList"? } (pending or not-yet-sent scheduled call; 400 when it is not waiting or has nobody to send to), GET GetAllPendingScheduledCalls, PUT UpdateScheduledDispatchTime. Scheduled: DispatchOn (department local time) or DispatchOnUtc (UTC; wins when both are sent). POST ReleaseUnit { "CallId", "UnitId", "Notify" } and POST ReleasePersonnel { "CallId", "UserId", "Notify" } release one resource from an open call (400 with the reason when it can't). EditCall accepts a pending call and notifies nobody; CloseCall takes close states 1–7 and SendNotification, and returns 400 while an incident command is active. Integrations can use a department API key instead of a member's login.
